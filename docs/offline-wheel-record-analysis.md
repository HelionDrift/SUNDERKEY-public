# Supplied Wheel ZIP and RECORD integrity

This additive R0 component checks internal byte consistency of a caller-supplied Wheel archive. It neither extracts nor installs a member. Its registered ownership is AOE-GC-D17-S05, capability `army.gc.d17.s05`, vector AV-GC-D17-S05-01, package IWP2-AOE-GC-D17-S05, wave IW2-017, commander AOE-GC-D17-CMD, group AOE-HQ-05, independent validator AOE-HQ-19, and blueprint `sha256:99ab56523d8c7702e8ffd8d5000755f794c03ed5e353113fb582539f787dfb20`. This is partial A7.4 evidence only, not specialist qualification or execution approval.

The closed model has exactly `schema_version: 1`, a bounded `source_id`, a simple five-component `wheel_filename`, and canonical padded standard `wheel_base64`. The model retains the complete original archive bytes. This deliberately narrow filename profile accepts a lowercase ASCII distribution component and dotted numeric version without a build tag. Other legal Wheel names are not thereby invalid. The report retains the complete original model and is not a sanitized publication artifact.

Public entry points are `analyze_supplied_wheel_record(model)`, `analyze_supplied_wheel_record_bytes(*, model_bytes)`, and `verify_supplied_wheel_record_analysis(report, *, model)`. `WheelRecordAnalysis` offers direct construction and `from_dict`, `from_json`, `to_dict`, and `to_json`. Every path reconstructs the full report from its retained source. The separate-original verifier also reconstructs the separately supplied source in the same operation and compares the complete results. The helper `component_fixture()` returns fresh, inert, synthetic stored-Wheel bytes. Addresses use separate model and report domains; they prove deterministic replay of supplied bytes, not their origin.

## Supported observations

The ZIP subset has one bounded single-disk end record and central directory, no hidden prefix, trailing bytes or unaddressed local records, and exact addressed member extents. Each central member is joined to its local header and checked for name, method, flags, CRC and sizes. For general-purpose bit 3, zero local placeholders are required and a data descriptor with or without its optional signature is checked at the compressed-data boundary. Ambiguous descriptor interpretations are refused. Stored and raw DEFLATE methods are decoded only within explicit incremental expanded-output and work ceilings. The analysis compares each member's expanded size and ZIP CRC-32 with its declared ZIP values.

The path subset accepts bounded printable ASCII relative forward-slash member names. Exact and case-fold duplicate paths, empty or dot components, backslashes, device/drive separators, directories and nonregular members are refused to avoid ambiguous identities. Unicode names, Unicode-path overrides, ZIP64, encryption, other compression, extra fields, multidisk and installation semantics are unsupported by this software profile, not declared invalid for all Wheels. A syntactically valid Wheel outside the subset may be refused without a universal validity conclusion.

The Wheel must contain exactly one expected `.dist-info/` trio of `METADATA`, `WHEEL`, and `RECORD`. RECORD is decoded as UTF-8 CSV with exactly three fields per row, respecting quoted commas and quotes. For this subset, every ordinary archive member appears once in RECORD and every ordinary RECORD row names one archive member. Every non-RECORD ordinary row carries canonical unpadded URL-safe `sha256=` encoding of a 32-byte digest and its exact decimal expanded byte size. The Wheel specification permits secure algorithms stronger than SHA-256; they are unsupported here, not necessarily erroneous. RECORD itself has empty hash and size. Deprecated `RECORD.jws` and `RECORD.p7s` may be present but must be omitted from RECORD; their bytes are retained without signature verification. This Wheel-specific required-hash rule is stricter than the separate installed-project RECORD format, whose hash and size fields may be absent.

`internal_integrity_matches` means the declared ZIP CRCs, expanded sizes, RECORD digests and RECORD sizes match these exact supplied bytes. A bad CRC or digest yields `internal_integrity_mismatch` with per-member evidence. CRC-32 is not a secure content digest. A party able to replace both payload and RECORD coherently can produce a matching report. No authentication, publisher identity, signature truth, compatibility, metadata semantics, safe installation, package behavior, vulnerability status or current repository state follows.
